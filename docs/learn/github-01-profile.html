GitHub 1: Your profile as a portfolio

Recruiters, clients and collaborators look at your GitHub profile before they read your CV. In about ten seconds they decide whether to dig deeper. This lesson makes those ten seconds count.

The profile README

GitHub has a hidden feature: create a public repository with exactly the same name as your username, add a README.md, and it appears at the top of your profile.

  1. Click New repository and name it your username exactly (for octocat, the repo is octocat).
  2. GitHub shows a note: "You found a secret!". Make it Public and tick Add a README file.
  3. Edit the README. That's your profile intro.

What makes a good one:

  • One line on who you are and what you build. "Web developer building accessible WordPress plugins" beats a wall of emoji.
  • What you're working on now, with links.
  • How to reach you, or where your work lives (site, portfolio, socials).
  • Optional: a short list of tools you actually use.

Keep it skimmable. Big animated banners and twenty badge images look busy. A few sentences that sound like you look professional.

Pin your best work

You can pin up to six repositories or gists to your profile: your profile → Customize your pins.

Pick repos that:

  • show the kind of work you want more of
  • have a clear README with a screenshot or demo link (see Lesson 6)
  • have a description and topics filled in (the gear icon next to About on the repo page)

A few polished projects beat forty half-finished ones. You can archive old experiments (Settings → General → Archive this repository) so they're clearly marked as finished.

The contribution graph

The green squares show days you contributed: commits to default branches, issues, pull requests, reviews and discussions. A few honest points:

  • It measures activity, not skill. Nobody serious judges you by an unbroken green streak.
  • Private work counts if you turn on Include private contributions on my profile (see Lesson 0).
  • Scripts that make fake commits to paint patterns are the same kind of inauthentic activity FAIR-PLAY.md warns about. Don't.

Fill in the basics

Settings → Public profile:

  • A real photo or a consistent avatar you use elsewhere
  • Name, a one-line bio, location, and your website
  • Pronouns and company, if you want them shown
  • Social accounts: link the ones you'd want a client to see

Exercise

  1. Create your profile README repo and write three sentences: who you are, what you're working on, how to reach you.
  2. Pin your three best repos, and make sure each has a description, topics and a README.
  3. Look at your profile in a private window. Would a stranger know what you do in ten seconds?

Learners Wall: add a link to your profile in your page's About me section (it counts as one of your three links).

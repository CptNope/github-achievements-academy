Fair Play: earning badges without risking your account

GitHub is where many of us keep our work, our portfolios and our businesses. Losing an account over a badge would be a terrible trade. This page explains where the line is.

This is a plain-English summary, not legal advice. The source of truth is always GitHub's Acceptable Use Policies and the Community Guidelines. Read them yourself, since they can change.

What the policies prohibit (paraphrased)

GitHub's Acceptable Use Policies have a section on spam and inauthentic activity. It forbids, among other things:

  • Automated excessive bulk activity and coordinated inauthentic activity, such as spamming.
  • Inauthentic interactions, like fake/sock-puppet accounts and automated inauthentic activity.
  • Rank abuse, like automated starring or following.
  • Secondary markets for inauthentic activity, such as buying or selling stars or followers.

GitHub keeps full discretion to remove content or suspend accounts that break these rules.

Green light: do this

ActivityWhy it's fine
Opening and quickly closing an issue in your own repo because you made a mistake or it was a duplicateThat's normal triage. It happens every day.
Merging your own PR without review in your own solo projectSolo developers do this all the time.
Real PRs that fix typos, docs, bugs or tests in other projectsThat's exactly what open source wants.
Adding Co-authored-by for someone who actually helped write the codeThat's accurate credit.
Answering Discussions questions you actually know the answer toThat's real help.
Sharing your project in communities where it's relevantThat's normal promotion.
Sponsoring a maintainer, even for $1That's real support.

Red light: never do this

ActivityWhy it's a problem
Scripts that open and merge hundreds or thousands of empty PRsAutomated excessive bulk activity
Alt accounts that star your repos, or answer and accept your own DiscussionsFake accounts and inauthentic interactions
"Star for star" / "follow for follow" exchanges, or buying starsRank abuse and secondary markets
Co-authored-by trailers for people who didn't contributeIt falsifies authorship, and it's dishonest
Spamming low-effort PRs to strangers' repos (e.g. whitespace changes)It wastes maintainers' time. Many projects ban accounts that do this.
Asking people in this repo to star or follow youPlease don't. Those PRs will be closed.

A rule of thumb

Would you be comfortable if a hiring manager or a GitHub staff member read the activity log?

If the answer is yes, you're fine. Badges should describe what you did, not be the reason you did it.

Why this repo has no "auto-farm" mode

Even the self-serve badges (Quickdraw, YOLO) are taught one action at a time, with an explanation, in your own sandbox. The coach.sh script deliberately does not loop. It does one thing, explains it, and stops. Bulk automation is the exact behavior the policies call out.
